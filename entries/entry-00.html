Diner Menu, Hand-Lettered Specials Board

Chalk-drawn condensed sans, likely freehand rather than a stencil — the counters wobble just enough to read as human. Heavy weight up top for the dish names, a lighter, looser script underneath for prices, so the eye lands on food first, cost second. Tight line spacing keeps a long list from feeling empty on the board, and the slight upward slope of each line gives it energy instead of looking sloppy. It's a reminder that hierarchy doesn't need three typefaces — weight and scale alone can carry the whole system.
